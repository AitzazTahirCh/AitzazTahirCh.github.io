Point of View

Most AI does not fail in the demo. It fails on the second day of real users.

The industry keeps funding demo skills, while production is a different job entirely: reliability, cost control, evals, approval gates, and designing for the day the model, the vendor or the network goes down. Every agent system I ship gets three brakes before it is allowed near real customers.

01

The Budget Brake

Every agent loop gets a hard budget: max steps, max tokens, max spend per run, and it stops when it hits the limit. Without it, an agent that retries or loops quietly burns money while looking busy. Invisible at demo scale; at millions of users, it is a bill.

Stops at the limit, every run.
02

The Checking Brake

Evals on outputs, fallbacks for when the model, the vendor or the network goes down, and a path that catches a bad output before a customer ever sees it. Checks that keep working after launch, not just before it.

Nothing bad reaches a customer.
03

The Permission Brake

The agent may act, but sensitive actions wait for a human yes: sending, spending, deleting, anything that touches an account or a customer. Human in the loop by design, not by accident.

No yes, no action.
